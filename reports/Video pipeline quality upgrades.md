# Swap the image model, then distil everything

**Urgent: the image model neuro-channel uses now, Qwen-Image-2.1, cannot legally make images for a monetized channel.** It is released under the Qwen *Research* License, which allows use "for research or evaluation purposes only". The licence has no carve-out for outputs and points commercial users to a separate paid licence ([Qwen-Image-2.1 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)). The good news is that the swap costs almost nothing in quality. On the Artificial Analysis open-weights arena, **FLUX 3 Image Klein scores Elo 1043 against Qwen-Image-2.1's 1042**. Klein is free for commercial use by creators under US$5M revenue, and it is a smaller, faster model ([AA image arena](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image?open-weights=true); [FLUX 3 Klein LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/flux-3-image-klein/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). After the licence fix, the biggest quality-per-dollar lever is **4-step distillation of Wan2.2-I2V-A14B**, which is Apache-2.0 like the base model. That should cut the measured ~900 s, ~$0.99 hero clip to an *estimated* 30–150 s, about $0.03–0.17 ([lightx2v distills](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models)). Narration is the next biggest gap. Chatterbox sits about **250 Elo below Fish Audio Drama 3** on the open TTS arena, and Drama 3 is licence-clean for creators under US$500k revenue ([AA TTS open-weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights)). The cheapest durable gain after that is a QC layer: best-of-N images, a Whisper WER gate and a VLM rubric. Put together, a licence-clean 15-min 1080p video should cost an *estimated* **$2.5–5.5 in GPU and API spend**. The current stack costs roughly $18 at 15 hero clips. A max-quality, API-heavy variant comes to about $20–50. Almost every speed figure for the new stack is an extrapolation, so the action plan starts with a one-day Modal benchmark.

**How to read the numbers.** **[MEAS]** means a published measurement on named hardware, or your own measured runtime. **[EST]** means an extrapolation in the research notes or in this report. **[VENDOR]** means an unverified claim by the model author. Modal costs per second: A10G $0.000306, A100-40 $0.000583, A100-80 $0.000694, H100 $0.001097. Licence verdicts come from `licences.md`, which read each primary licence text on 2026-10-29. Where other notes disagree, that file wins. This is not legal advice.

## Qwen-Image-2.1 is research-only, and FLUX 3 Klein replaces it at equal Elo

Qwen-Image-2.1 and its 8-step Turbo sibling ship under a byte-identical "Qwen RESEARCH LICENSE AGREEMENT" dated 20 Sep 2026. Section 2(a) grants rights "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL PURPOSES ONLY". Section 2(b) forbids "any commercial purpose without obtaining a separate commercial license" ([LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE); [Turbo LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo/blob/main/LICENSE)). Some commentators quote a Qwen post on X saying "outputs are not part of the licensed Materials". That post never mentions commercial use. The Hugging Face thread asking for clarity was still open, and users report that the commercial-licence address bounces ([Latenode](https://latenode.com/ai-trends/qwen-image-2-1-commercial-licence); [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1/discussions/6)). A social-media post does not amend a licence. Running the model to make images for a revenue-earning channel is the commercial use the text prohibits. The prompt-enhancer repos and the lightx2v INT8 Turbo build carry the same restriction. A third-party GGUF re-upload tagged Apache-2.0 cannot relicense the weights either ([HF API listing](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=lightx2v)). There are two practical steps. First, stop generating with it now. Second, decide with your own counsel what to do about any images already published, for example re-rendering those scenes. The official route to keep it is to get a commercial licence from model-business@notice.qwencloud.com.

Speed figures are thin. BFL reports **0.55 s per 1024² image on a GB300** and 8.9 s on an RTX 4070 for the distilled model [MEAS, vendor hardware]. On a Modal A100-40, an *estimated* 3–8 s per image would replace the measured ~60 s for Qwen-Image-2.1. That frees enough budget to generate four candidates per scene and still spend less than today. Native multi-reference editing in Klein and HiDream-O1 (up to 10 references) has largely replaced IP-Adapter and PuLID for keeping one protagonist consistent. A fixed character sheet with front, three-quarter and profile views, passed as 2–4 references per scene, gives per-video consistency. A style LoRA trained on 20–40 curated stills gives consistency across videos. Ostris AI Toolkit supports Klein LoRA training from day one, and comparable LoRA runs cost under about $15 ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein); [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/fine-tune-flux2-wan-lora-cost-gpu-cloud-2026/)). For thumbnails, the closed leaders sit 130–180 Elo above any open model. Nano Banana 2.1 scores 1176 at about $0.034 per image, and GPT Image 2.5 scores 1222 at about $0.21 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image?open-weights=true)). A few API thumbnail candidates per video cost cents. Add headline text afterwards with a real font in PIL, so the spelling is always correct.

Wan 2.5, 2.6 and 3.0 went API-only, which leaves Wan2.2 (Apache-2.0) as the last open Wan flagship ([atlascloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/ja/blog/tips/is-wan-3.0-open-source)). The only open model that clearly beats it on the AA I2V arena is **MiniMax H3**, at #4 with Elo 1128, above Veo 3.1 and Kling 3.0. Its licence makes it **NOT OK** for a monetized channel. The licence excludes the US, EU, UK and South Korea. §V.4 also forbids anyone to "display… any of their Outputs… outside the Applicable Territory", and a public YouTube video is displayed in all four ([MiniMax H3 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/LICENSE); [AA I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Modal runs in US and EU regions, so self-hosting it there would breach the licence twice over.

The distillation case rests on step arithmetic plus one vendor measurement. The lightx2v card reports **720p I2V in 26.7 s at 4 steps against ~2,685 s at 40 steps**, on an RTX 5090 using NVFP4 and sparse attention ([LightWan2.2-A14B](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/LightWan2.2-A14B)). That exact build needs Blackwell, which none of your Modal GPU types are. The usable path is the FP8 (`scaled_fp8_e4m3`) or BF16 4-step experts. The schedule runs 2 steps on the high-noise expert, then 2 on the low-noise expert, driven by LightX2V's `run_wan22_moe_i2v_distill.sh`. You must point `model_path` at the full `Wan-AI/Wan2.2-I2V-A14B` directory, because the distill repo ships only DiT weights. The two notes disagree on H100 timing: one estimates 30–60 s, the other 60–150 s. Use 30–150 s until you have measured it. Two hardware facts set the GPU choice. Ampere (A10G and both A100s) has no FP8 tensor cores, so FP8 checkpoints save memory there but add no speed. Feature caching such as TeaCache or MagCache gains little once only 4 steps remain. The H100 is therefore the natural target, and an A100-80 is only worth it if it runs less than about 1.6× slower.

LTX-2.5 is the strongest second engine. The distilled 22B DiT runs a fixed 8 steps at CFG 1. It renders natively at 24 fps, and its x2 latent upsampler turns a 544×960 first stage into 1088×1920 in one pass, so LTX shots skip both FlashVSR and frame interpolation. The repeatable `--image PATH FRAME_IDX STRENGTH` flag lets one clip start from a still and settle on another ([LTX-2.5 card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)). Its arena Elo of about 910 sits roughly 90 below Kling 3.0. The arena prompts lean toward people and cinematic scenes, though, so the gap on abstract brain b-roll is unknown. Its licence has one subtle duty: you must not "remove, disable, alter, or circumvent" watermarking or provenance metadata, and must make "reasonable efforts to use the latest version" ([LICENSE-2_x](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/blob/main/LICENSE-2_x)). It is ambiguous whether an ffmpeg re-encode that drops embedded metadata breaches this. Lightricks released a `LTX-2-Marking` repo on 28 Oct 2026, and it is worth reading before you ship LTX shots.

Long-video methods matter less than they appear. A 40–80-scene explainer is a chain of separate 5–10 s shots. The useful tools are first/last-frame conditioning, LTX keyframes, and native multi-shot for keeping a character consistent across cuts. Autoregressive minute-scale generators such as Self-Forcing++ (up to 4 min 15 s) generalise poorly outside their training lane ([Self-Forcing++](https://huggingface.co/papers/2510.02283); [atlascloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/th/blog/guides/long-video-generation-blog-1)). Chaining clips by conditioning on the last frame builds up colour drift after 2–3 hops, so keep one generation per scene. HoloCine-style multi-shot is research-grade and needs FlashAttention-3 on H100-class hardware ([HoloCine](https://github.com/yihao-meng/HoloCine)).

Post-processing is cheap but full of licence and hardware traps. **FlashVSR v1.1** (Apache-2.0) runs at about **17 fps for 768×1408 output on an A100** [MEAS]. Its Block-Sparse-Attention is verified on Ampere, with only limited acceleration on Hopper, so run it on an A100, not an H100. It is tuned for 4× upscaling, so for 720p to 1080p you must downscale either the input or the output. Some early ComfyUI ports fall back to dense attention and lose quality ([FlashVSR GitHub](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR)). Use **SeedVR2** only for hero shots: one Comfy benchmark measured 312 s against FlashVSR's 41 s for the same 10 s clip ([Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/video-upscale)). For Wan's 16 fps output, interpolate with **RIFE (MIT)**. The video notes suggested GIMM-VFI as a higher-quality option, but its S-Lab License 1.0 is **non-commercial only**, so it is **NOT OK** ([GIMM-VFI LICENSE](https://github.com/GSeanCDAT/GIMM-VFI/blob/main/LICENSE); [Practical-RIFE LICENSE](https://github.com/hzwer/Practical-RIFE/blob/main/LICENSE)).

Closed APIs change the economics of hero shots. On the AA arena, **HeyGen Video 1 (Elo 1119) and Agnes-Video-2.5 (1103) cost about $0.20 per 5 s**. Both beat Kling 3.0 (1000) and Veo 3.1 (956). The MiniMax H3 API costs about $0.40 per clip at Elo 1128, and Seedance 2.5 leads at 1152 for about $2.84 ([AA I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). The current unaccelerated Wan clip at ~$0.99 costs more than these top-10 APIs. The licence notes did not review any of their API terms, including MiniMax's, whose Q&A only says the API is "globally available" ([H3 Q&A](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3/blob/main/docs/QA-about-License.md)). Read each one's commercial-use and output clauses before production use.

The long-form recipe is consistent across vendors. Supply the exact transcript of the reference clip: Higgs says it "materially improves" cloning, and Breeze says a wrong transcript degrades output ([Higgs card](https://huggingface.co/bosonai/higgs-tts-3-4b)). Chunk at sentence boundaries. Re-transcribe every chunk with faster-whisper and retry above a WER threshold. The ~5–10% threshold is a practitioner convention that needs tuning. Then master once. In the notes' ffmpeg pattern, narration keys `sidechaincompress` on the music bed (threshold ~0.02, ratio ~6–10), then two-pass `loudnorm` targets about −14 LUFS integrated with a true peak of −1.5 dBTP ([skills.sh mixing patterns](https://skills.sh/yonatangross/orchestkit/audio-mixing-patterns); [DEV loudnorm guide](https://dev.to/javidjamae/ffmpeg-loudnorm-filter-ebu-r128-loudness-normalization-guide-15d4)).

MMAudio's weights are CC-BY-NC, and ThinkSound is research-only ([MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio); [ThinkSound](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/ThinkSound)). ## Automatic QC and a durable workflow layer fix most of the remaining quality problems

Model upgrades raise the quality ceiling. QC raises the floor, which is what viewers see in the weakest of 60 scenes. The literature supports a layered design. ViMax "generates 2 keyframe candidates in parallel and selects one using your vision-capable chat model" ([ViMax](https://github.com/HKUDS/ViMax)). TIGER-Lab's VideoScore2 scores visual quality, text alignment and physical consistency, and its authors show it working as a best-of-N reward model ([arXiv 2509.22799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22799)). VBench-I2V provides subject-consistency and motion-smoothness evaluators for image-to-video output ([VBench](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench)). The most transferable pattern for explainer content is **Code2Video**. A planner, a Manim coder with auto-fix, and a VLM critic using "visual anchor prompts" reported about **40% improvement over direct code generation**. Its TeachQuiz metric checks whether the video actually teaches ([arXiv 2510.01174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01174)). Animated Manim diagrams render on CPU, so the free 4-core ARM VM can produce them. They also fill the gap Ken Burns stills leave for mechanisms like synaptic transmission.

The recommended ladder runs in four tiers. For images, generate 4 FLUX 3 Klein candidates in one batched Modal call and score them with HPSv3 or ImageReward. Send the top two to a VLM rubric that returns JSON with fields for artefacts, garbled text, and matches-prompt and matches-narration scores. Run Qwen3-VL on Modal for bulk filtering and Claude vision for semantic checks. Regenerate at most twice, then fall back to a Manim scene. For hero clips, run the rubric on 8–16 sampled frames plus VideoScore2. For voice, use the Whisper WER gate, a duration-per-character outlier check and optional ECAPA speaker similarity. For the whole video, Claude reviews a contact sheet showing one frame and its narration line per scene. The notes give no measured threshold or cost for any of these, so log every score to the HF dataset and tune the gates against your own rejections. Avoid DOVER and FAST-VQA: both are stale and trained on user-generated content ([DOVER](https://github.com/VQAssessment/DOVER)).

On the ARM VM, keep Modal as the GPU executor and add a lightweight durable layer. **DBOS** is a library, not a server. It is backed by Postgres and has queue concurrency caps and rate limits. **Huey with SQLite** is the minimal alternative. Temporal and Hatchet each bring a server and are more operations work than a single free VM justifies ([DBOS queues](https://docs.dbos.dev/python/examples/queue-patterns); [Huey](https://github.com/coleifer/huey)). Modal already provides per-input retries with exponential backoff, and `.map(return_exceptions=True)` ([Modal retries](https://modal.com/docs/guide/retries)). Make each stage a function of `(scene_id, inputs_hash)` that writes its artifact plus `qc.json`. The MCP server's `rerun_scene` then becomes idempotent, and a spend ledger can refuse work past a per-video cap. No native Modal budget cap was found in the notes. Cold start deserves batching rather than tuning. Modal's GPU snapshots are alpha and give "no speedup when weight loading from storage dominates", which is the case for the 30–70 GB video stacks ([Modal snapshots](https://modal.com/docs/guide/memory-snapshot)). Process all of one video's clips in a single warm container session. After upload, YouTube's Test & Compare tests up to three titles or thumbnails and picks the winner by **watch-time share, not CTR**. ## Conclusion

The 2026 pattern is that open-weight quality now arrives with conditions attached. Revenue caps (US$500k for Fish, US$5M for BFL, US$10M for LTX), attribution lines, watermark-preservation clauses and territory-plus-display bans now decide which models a monetized channel can use, more than arena rank does. The channel's real constraint is therefore licence hygiene at the pipeline level. Keep a per-model licence manifest in the HF dataset, generate the description credits automatically ("Built with Fish Audio", "Boson AI's Higgs Audio", "Powered by Stability AI"), keep the provenance metadata, and re-check licences whenever a model is bumped. Qwen's quiet switch from Apache-2.0 to a research licence between 2512 and 2.1 shows that a version bump can silently change the legal status of a pipeline.

The second shift is economic. Distillation and cheap top-10 APIs have turned the hero clip from the most expensive scene type into a minor line item. Spend now buys candidates and judgment: best-of-N, VLM critics, and a human approval step that also meets FLUX's review clause. The biggest open uncertainty is not model choice but transfer. Every leaderboard cited here is built on people-heavy, cinematic prompts, and none measures abstract neuroscience b-roll, diagrams or 15-minute narration drift. The one-day benchmark on the channel's own prompts is worth more than any further desk research.
